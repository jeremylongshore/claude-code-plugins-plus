# Negotiation -- Chris Voss Tactical Empathy Implementation Guide

## Overview

Apply FBI hostage negotiator Chris Voss's techniques (tactical empathy, mirroring, labeling, calibrated questions, Ackerman method) to salary, contract, and business negotiations.

## Core Techniques

### 1. Mirroring

Repeat the last 1-3 words of what the other party just said. Encourages elaboration without revealing your position.

```
Them: "We can't go above $120k for this role."
You: "Can't go above $120k?"
Them: [elaborates, often revealing flexibility or constraints]
```

### 2. Labeling

Name the emotion you sense. Creates empathy and defuses tension.

**Format:** "It seems like..." / "It sounds like..." / "It feels like..."

```
Them: [seems frustrated] "We've been going back and forth on this for weeks."
You: "It sounds like you're exhausted by how long this is taking."
Them: "Yeah, honestly. We just want to get this done."
```

### 3. Calibrated Questions

"How" and "What" questions make the other party solve your problems.

**Avoid:** "Why?" (sounds accusatory), "Can you?" (yes/no trap)

**Use instead:**
- "How am I supposed to do that?"
- "What would need to happen for that to work?"
- "How does this affect the rest of your team?"
- "What's the biggest challenge you face here?"

### 4. The Accusation Audit

List every negative thing they might be thinking about you. Say them out loud first. Defuses them.

```
"I imagine you're skeptical about the ROI given you've tried similar tools before and been disappointed."
```

### 5. The Ackerman Method (Salary/Price Negotiation)

```markdown
## Ackerman Negotiation Script

1. Set your target price
2. First offer: 65% of target
3. If rejected: 85% of target
4. If rejected: 95% of target
5. Final offer: 100% of target (add a non-monetary item to signal you're at the limit)

Note: Odd numbers feel more calculated and credible.
Example: $113,450 feels more researched than $115,000.
```

### 6. The "That's Right" Moment

Your goal is "that's right" (genuine agreement), NOT "you're right" (dismissal to end the conversation).

**Technique:** Summarize their perspective so accurately and empathetically that they say "that's right."
