# Influence Psychology -- Implementation Guide

## Overview

Apply Robert Cialdini's seven principles of influence (Reciprocity, Commitment/Consistency, Social Proof, Authority, Liking, Scarcity, Unity) in ethical product, marketing, and sales contexts.

## The Seven Principles

### 1. Reciprocity

Give first. People feel obligated to return favors.

**Applications:**
- Offer a free tool, resource, or audit before asking for anything
- Send a personalized handwritten note before a cold outreach
- Add unexpected value (over-deliver on early deliverables)

**Template:**
```
Trigger: User signs up
Action: Immediately send a curated "getting started" resource pack
Why: Creates felt obligation to engage, reduces churn
```

### 2. Commitment & Consistency

Small commitments lead to larger ones. Public commitments are stickiest.

**Applications:**
- Onboarding: "What is your #1 goal for using this?" (micro-commitment)
- Ask for a small "yes" before a big ask
- Use progress bars to create completion commitment

### 3. Social Proof

People look to others' behavior when uncertain.

```markdown
## Social Proof Hierarchy (strongest to weakest)

1. Expert endorsement (named expert in their field)
2. Celebrity/influencer (relevant to audience)
3. User testimonials with specific results ("saved 4 hours/week")
4. Aggregate numbers ("10,000+ teams use this")
5. Case studies with named companies
6. Star ratings / review counts
```

### 4. Authority

Credentials, titles, and expertise signals reduce resistance.

**Quick authority builders:**
- Author bio with specific credentials (not just "expert")
- Named press mentions ("As seen in Forbes")
- Specific data citations ("per our study of 500 customers")
- Certifications and awards visible at decision points

### 5. Liking

People say yes to those they like. Similarity, compliments, cooperation.

**Applications:**
- Mirror language/vocabulary of your target persona
- Share founder story (human, relatable, not corporate)
- Highlight shared values explicitly
- Genuine praise before persuasion attempts

### 6. Scarcity

Less available = more desirable.

**Ethical scarcity (based on reality, not manufactured fear):**
```markdown
True scarcity: "Limited beta spots (12 remaining)"
Time-based: "Early bird pricing ends [specific date]"
Capacity: "We only take 5 new clients per month"
```

### 7. Unity

Shared identity creates compliance. "We are the same."

**Applications:**
- Target specific identity groups ("for indie founders")
- Co-creation / community involvement
- "Us vs. them" framing with a common external enemy (the problem, not competitors)

## Influence Audit for Landing Pages

```markdown
Rate each principle 0-2:
0 = Not present
1 = Present but weak
2 = Strong and specific

Reciprocity: ___ (free value given before ask?)
Commitment: ___ (micro-commitment in flow?)
Social Proof: ___ (specific, credible, visible?)
Authority: ___ (credentials shown at right moment?)
Liking: ___ (human, relatable, shared values?)
Scarcity: ___ (real scarcity communicated?)
Unity: ___ (identity group addressed?)

Score /14. Under 8: major opportunity.
```
